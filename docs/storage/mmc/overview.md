# Overview

This section provides a comprehensive introduction to the Embedded Multi-Media Card (eMMC) and Multi-Media Card (MMC) technologies, covering their historical background, core features, and the evolution of the MMC/eMMC standard versions.

## Background

The Multi-Media Card (MMC) was born in 1997 as a storage card standard jointly developed by SanDisk and Siemens. It is a type of flash memory card used for storing multimedia data such as images, audio, and video, applied in early digital cameras, mobile phones, and portable media players. 

The Embedded Multi-Media Card (eMMC), introduced by SanDisk in 2006, is a storage solution that integrates MMC technology directly into the device's interior. Compared to Secure Digital (SD) which deeply penetrated the mobile sector, eMMC primarily targets the embedded storage field, offering lower power consumption and cost advantages, making it widely applied in mid-to-low-end smartphones, tablet computers, digital cameras, and automotive electronics.

## Features

With its high integration, cost-effectiveness, stability, reliability, and low power consumption, eMMC is widely used in various embedded systems, providing a cost-effective solution for storage demands.

* **High Integration**: eMMC integrates the storage chip and controller, reducing the need for external components and simplifying system design. This makes eMMC a suitable solution for space-constrained and power-constrained devices.
* **Cost-Effectiveness**: Compared to other storage technologies such as Universal Flash Storage (UFS) and Solid-State Drive (SSD), eMMC holds a clear cost advantage, enabling its widespread use in many mid-to-low-end consumer electronics such as smartphones and tablet computers.
* **Reliability**: eMMC features built-in Error Correction Code (ECC), bad block management, and wear leveling functions, ensuring data integrity and long-term storage stability. This makes eMMC excel in applications with high demands for data reliability.
* **Low Power Consumption**: eMMC features a low-power design suitable for mobile devices and embedded systems requiring prolonged battery life. Its standby mode and low-power characteristics provide significant advantages in battery-driven equipment.
* **Standardization**: eMMC follows standards established by the solid-state storage standards organization JEDEC, possessing broad device compatibility and interoperability. It supports various operating systems and hardware platforms, ensuring cross-platform application flexibility.

## Standard

The MMC standard version phases progressed from the initial version 1.0 released in 1996 through multiple iterations. Version 4.3 introduced eMMC, shifting it into the embedded storage market, and version 4.5 eventually removed support for MMC. In 2008, the Multi-Media Card Association (MMCA) transferred all MMC specifications including eMMC, SecureMMC, and miCARD assets to the JEDEC organization dedicated to solid-state industry standardization.

* **Version 5.0 (2013)**: Added the HS400 mode, introducing dual-edge sampling on top of the HS200 mode, elevating the theoretical speed to 400 MB/s.
* **Version 5.1 (2015)**: Added the HS400 ES mode, improving command reception reliability on top of the HS400 mode.
