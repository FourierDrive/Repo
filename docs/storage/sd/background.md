# Background

The Secure Digital (SD) card (or SDMEM card / memory card) was jointly developed in 1999 by SanDisk, Panasonic, and Toshiba. It introduced higher storage capacities, read/write speeds, and added data encryption functions. It is widely used in digital cameras, smartphones, handheld devices, video game consoles, and various embedded systems. In 2000, the SD Association (SDA) was formed to develop and promote the card standards.

The Secure Digital Input/Output (SDIO) card was introduced in 2001 as an extension based on the SD card specification, incorporating I/O functions for devices requiring expanded capabilities, such as Wi-Fi and Bluetooth adapters, GPS modules, etc. The physical and electrical characteristics of the SDIO interface and the SD interface are identical; devices that can support SDIO cards can generally support SD memory cards, but not vice versa.
